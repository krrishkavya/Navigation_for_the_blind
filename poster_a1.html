1. Abstract

Independent outdoor mobility presents severe safety risks for Blind and Visually Impaired (BVI) pedestrians. Traditional embedded microcontrollers cannot support resource-heavy computer vision, monocular depth estimation, automatic speech recognition (ASR), and road routing within wearable Size, Weight, and Power (SWaP) constraints.

We present an end-to-end 5G-Enabled Edge Computing Assistive System deployed across the IIT Guwahati campus. Heterogeneous hardware on the pedestrian (chest-mounted Arduino UNO Q with HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor and USB camera, paired with a pocket smartphone) continuously streams compressed video, proximity telemetry, and satellite GNSS over high-speed 5G uplinks. A dedicated 5G Edge Server executes fused YOLOv8 object detection, MiDaS relative inverse depth maps, and geometric ground-plane homography with a temporal Rate-of-Closure (ROC) filter to suppress false alarms. A localized Whisper AI speech pipeline with token-based campus landmark fuzzy snapping enables hands-free voice routing to 75+ campus destinations with sub-50 ms alert latency.

2. Introduction

According to the WHO, over 2.2 billion people suffer from visual impairment. While white canes identify ground-level obstacles directly ahead, they fail to detect elevated barriers, low overhangs, and dynamic collision hazards like oncoming e-rickshaws, bicycles, and pedestrians.

Edge intelligence provides a transformative breakthrough: by leveraging 5G Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC) and Enhanced Mobile Broadband (eMBB), computation-heavy neural inference is relocated to a nearby edge workstation, achieving:

  • Thermal & Weight Minimization: Wearable runs cool without noisy fans or heavy battery packs.
  • High Frame Rate Perception: 25 FPS vision inference at sub-50 ms round-trip warning latency.
  • True Multi-Modal Feedback: Earphone audio guidance, earcon warning tones, and haptic vibration pulses.

3. Objectives

  • Heterogeneous Wearable Ingestion: Stream video (480x320 @ 25 FPS) and ultrasonic telemetry from an Arduino UNO Q dual-core MPU/MCU client.
  • Blind-Zone Ultrasonic Guard: Eliminate the physical <3 cm HC-SR04 dead zone using stateful hysteresis to detect touching barriers.
  • False-Alarm Suppression: Implement Ground-Plane Projective Homography and Rate-of-Closure (ROC) velocity gating.
  • Hands-Free Speech Navigation: Whisper AI transcribing colloquial queries snapped to 75+ IIT Guwahati campus pin-drops.
  • Zero-Touch Autonomous Cloud Sync: Auto-discover ephemeral cloud tunnels across independent networks via ntfy.sh heartbeat broker.

4. System Model & Formulation

1. 5G Edge Offloading Latency Model: Total latency $T_{\text{total}}$ must satisfy the pedestrian safety threshold ($T_{\text{total}} \le 120\text{ ms}$ at 1.4 m/s walking speed):

$$T_{\text{total}} = T_{\text{enc}} + T_{\text{uplink}} + T_{\text{YOLO+MiDaS}} + T_{\text{downlink}} + T_{\text{audio}} \approx 48.6\text{ ms}$$

2. Ground-Plane Projective Homography: For camera height $h = 1.35\text{ m}$, focal length $f_y = 0.85 \cdot H$, and horizon line $y_{\text{horizon}} = 0.40 \cdot H$, the physical distance to obstacle base $y_{\text{base}}$ is:

$$d_{\text{homography}} = \frac{h \cdot f_y}{\max(1.0, y_{\text{base}} - y_{\text{horizon}})}$$

3. Dynamic Rate-of-Closure (ROC) Gating: Avoids nuisance beeping for static walls by validating approaching velocity:

$$\text{ROC}(t) = \frac{d(t - \Delta t) - d(t)}{\Delta t}; \quad \text{Alert} = \left(\text{ROC} \ge 0.4\text{ m/s}\right) \land \left(v_{\text{walk}} \ge 1.0\text{ km/h}\right)$$

A. Heterogeneous Dual-Core Wearable (Node 1)

The Zephyr MCU core runs non-blocking ultrasonic pulses (16 Hz). A Blind-Zone Guard checks: if previous distance was <18 cm and echo drops to zero, the object is touching (locked at 2.0 cm). The Linux MPU core reads proximity over arduino.router_bridge RPC, compresses camera frames (JPEG Q=65), and uplinks via raw RFC 6455 WebSockets.

B. Spatial Perception & ROC Threat Gating (Node 3)

YOLOv8 filters pedestrian collision hazards (vehicles, bicycles, people) within an active corridor (15% to 85% frame width). Fused with MiDaS inverse depth maps and homography, the Rate-of-Closure filter ensures audio warnings only sound when the user is actively closing in on a hazard.

C. Speech Navigation & Campus Snapping

Phone captures 16 kHz mono PCM audio. The edge server's local Whisper AI model transcribes queries, applies regex query sanitization, and snaps colloquial Indian English terms (e.g., "Brahma", "Dhansiri", "Library") to canonical landmarks using RapidFuzz matching. OSRM computes turn-by-turn walking steps and Leaflet GeoJSON routes with a 35 km spatial ceiling.

6. Experimental Setup

Field trials were conducted across pedestrian pathways and roads at IIT Guwahati (Hostel sector, Academic Complex, and Commercial Hub):

  • Wearable Hardware: Arduino UNO Q, HC-SR04 Proximity Sensor, 120° FOV USB CMOS camera.
  • Mobile Device: 5G-enabled smartphone running Web Audio Client and fine GNSS receiver.
  • Edge Compute Platform: Workstation equipped with PyTorch 2.5, CUDA acceleration, YOLOv8 medium, Intel MiDaS v2.1, and OpenAI Whisper ('small').
  • Connectivity: 5G Commercial Carrier Hotspot + Cloudflare Tunnel + ntfy.sh autonomous discovery.

Key Architectural Findings:

  • Acoustic Fatigue Elimination: Combining Doppler walking speed ($v \ge 1.0\text{ km/h}$) with Rate-of-Closure successfully eliminated false alerts when standing next to walls or waiting at road intersections.
  • Zero-Touch Cloud Sync: Wearable auto-discovered dynamic server tunnel addresses via public broadcast topic with zero App Lab intervention.
  • Precise Campus Geocoding: Resolved colloquial terms like "Brahma" directly to Brahmaputra Hostel $(26.1942, 91.6930)$ with complete Google Maps and OSRM walkway fidelity.

8. Conclusion

The proposed 5G-enabled wearable assistive system demonstrates that offloading computer vision, relative depth estimation, and natural speech transcription to a high-capacity edge node overcomes embedded hardware limitations. With sub-50 ms threat alerting, intelligent blind-zone ultrasonic guarding, and domain-specific speech routing, the system provides a robust, hands-free spatial mobility aid for BVI pedestrians on large university campuses.
